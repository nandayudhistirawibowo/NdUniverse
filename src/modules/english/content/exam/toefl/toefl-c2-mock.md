    represents a paradigm shift from classical computers. Classical machines process information using
    bits that are either 0 or 1. In contrast, quantum computers use quantum bits, or qubits, which can

    where qubits become interdependent regardless of distance—allows quantum computers to perform certain
    calculations exponentially faster. For instance, factoring large numbers, which underpins modern cryptography,
    could be solved in polynomial time on a sufficiently advanced quantum processor. However, building
    such machines is fraught with challenges. Qubits are extremely sensitive to environmental noise, leading
    to decoherence that destroys quantum information. Researchers are exploring error‑correction codes
    and topological qubits to mitigate these issues, but practical, large‑scale quantum computers remain
    a distant goal. Despite these hurdles, the potential applications span drug discovery, optimization
    problems, and simulation of complex quantum systems, making the field a hotbed of interdisciplinary
    research.
